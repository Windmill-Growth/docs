Open **Targeting** > **Intent signals** to review the Signal Brief that guides discovery.

Describe the problems, topics, or buying situations connected to your offer. Keep them specific enough to recognize in public activity. Review suggested topics before saving them.

## Configure the evidence you want

Review your business context, **Buyer pains**, **Buying triggers**, **Relevant topics**, and **Competitors**. Use **Relevance rules** to describe positive evidence and its weight. Save changes in the section you edit.

Use **Keywords & niches** for company terms that make a lead relevant. These terms complement the people and company criteria in [Lead criteria](/discovery/lead-criteria).

The **LinkedIn activity** section connects accounts and selects the activity you want to collect. Follow [LinkedIn activity](/discovery/linkedin-activity) to configure profile views, followers, connections, and company-page followers.

## Read a lead's signal

| Label | How to interpret it |
| --- | --- |
| **Warm** | A direct signal relevant to your offer. |
| **Related** | Contextual evidence related to your offer. |
| **Activity** | Observed activity with a weaker connection to your offer. |
| **ICP match** | Fit information without a recorded signal of the other types. |

These labels describe signal strength. They are separate from the ICP match score and the source that brought the person into traxy.

Open a lead's **Signals** tab to inspect recorded observations and available source links. Use the evidence actually shown when drafting a message. A missing signal does not prove that someone has no interest.

## Improve discovery

Change topics when the results consistently point to the wrong problem or audience. If the people themselves are wrong, revise [Lead criteria](/discovery/lead-criteria). Use [Tracking](/discovery/tracking) to add relevant profiles or specific posts.
